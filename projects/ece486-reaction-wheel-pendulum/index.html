Work / Reaction-wheel pendulum control

Course project · University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign · ECE 486 Control Systems · Fall 2024

System Identification, Observer-Based State Feedback, and Hybrid Swing-Up Control of a Reaction-Wheel Inverted Pendulum

Electromechanical system identification, pole-placement state feedback, a 4-state Luenberger velocity observer, and energy-injection swing-up control implemented and compared on a physical reaction-wheel inverted pendulum.

Platform
Physical reaction-wheel inverted pendulum with optical encoders and ±10 V DAC motor drive; MATLAB / Simulink
Role
Joint project with Romeo Delgado and Alexander Vega: electromechanical system identification, pole-placement and Luenberger observer synthesis, friction compensation, and hardware disturbance testing.
Key results
  • 6.45 vs 4.2 maximum pulsed disturbance held on the physical pendulum: state feedback vs. observer-based loop
Evaluation modality
hardware · simulation — hardware disturbance thresholds are recorded in laboratory-interface units from manual perturbation trials.

Overview

A reaction-wheel inverted pendulum stabilizes its upright equilibrium by accelerating an internal flywheel, making it a canonical benchmark for underactuated nonlinear control in the presence of Coulomb stiction, ±10 V DAC saturation, and velocity-estimation lag. Building on a semester sequence of six foundational control laboratories[5], this project encompasses full electromechanical parameter identification, linearized state-space and Luenberger observer synthesis, asymmetric friction feedforward, and hybrid energy-based swing-up stabilization on the physical pendulum rig (Figure 1)[1][2][4].

System identification & controller synthesis

  • Electromechanical characterization. Three dedicated identification experiments established the DC motor torque constant (kt = 0.0289 N·m/A), back-EMF constant (kb = 0.0365 V·s/rad), flywheel inertia (Jr = 5.137×10⁻⁴ kg·m²), viscous damping (br = 9.01×10⁻⁵ N·m·s), Coulomb stiction (τc = 2.63×10⁻³ N·m), and pendulum natural frequency (ωn = 7.72 rad/s; Figure 2b)[1].
  • Pole placement & Luenberger observer. Linearizing the Euler–Lagrange equations about the upright equilibrium (θp = π) yields an open-loop unstable plant with eigenvalues at {+9.54, −9.54, −0.175, 0} and verified 4-state controllability and 2-encoder observability. Full-state feedback places the closed-loop poles at {−6, −7, −8, 0} (K = [−24.573, −4.1614, 0, −0.0562]), paired with a 4-state Luenberger state observer with poles at {−20, −21, −22, −23} (Figure 2a)[2].
  • Hybrid swing-up & friction compensation. Outside the ±0.15 rad upright capture basin, an energy-injection pump drives the pendulum toward the homoclinic orbit before switching bumplessly to the linear stabilizer augmented with asymmetric Coulomb-plus-viscous friction feedforward[4].

Hardware disturbance evaluation

Evaluated on the physical pendulum across pulsed and constant external perturbations as well as initial angular/velocity offsets, direct state feedback sustained larger disturbances than the high-gain Luenberger observer loop (6.45 vs. 4.2 under pulsed disturbances and 6.8 vs. 4.75 under constant disturbances; Figure 3), isolating how unmodeled stiction deadzones and ±10 V actuator saturation interact with fast observer poles on hardware[3][4].

Limitations & scope

  • Laboratory-interface perturbation units: The hardware disturbance tolerance thresholds reported in Figure 3 (6.45 vs. 4.2 pulsed; 6.8 vs. 4.75 constant; 0.9 / 0.1 vs. 0.7 / 0.09 initial state offsets) were recorded in the Simulink hardware-in-the-loop interface’s native perturbation units during manual hardware testing without exported raw time-series logs or multi-trial confidence intervals[3].
