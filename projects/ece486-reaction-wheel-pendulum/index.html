Work / Reaction-wheel pendulum control

Course project · University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign · ECE 486 Control Systems · Fall 2024

Balancing a reaction-wheel pendulum on lab hardware

Model, identify and control an underactuated pendulum — friction-compensated state feedback, an observer and energy-based swing-up — compared on the physical rig. Measured state feedback held larger disturbances than the observer-based loop.

Platform
Reaction-wheel inverted pendulum on the course lab rig
My role
Team project with Romeo Delgado and Alexander Vega: modelling, identification, controller design and hardware testing were shared, and the report does not split them.
Key results
  • 6.45 vs 4.2 largest pulsed disturbance held: state feedback vs observer-based (lab units)
Evidence
hardware · simulation — robustness values are in lab-interface units from a small number of trials.

A reaction-wheel pendulum balances upside down by spinning a wheel; it is a standard underactuated-control benchmark, and on real hardware friction, motor saturation and model error show up immediately. The course set the platform and the stages; the modelling, identification, gains, observer and the hardware comparison are ours.

What we did. We derived and linearised a Lagrangian model about the upright position and confirmed controllability[1], fitted a Coulomb-plus-viscous friction model and compensated it, placed the closed-loop poles[2], added a Luenberger observer, and built an energy-based swing-up that hands over to the linear controller near the top[4].

Result. On the rig, state feedback from the measured states held larger disturbances than the observer-based loop, although the observer was stable in the linear model; sensitivity to unmodelled dynamics is the likely reason[3]. The most robust configuration combined state feedback, friction compensation and swing-up switching. The project built on a semester of labs on the same hardware family[5].

Limitation. The report calls the comparison subjective testing: values are in the lab interface’s units, with no raw logs or repeated-trial statistics. The course manual’s hardware photo and schematics are not reproduced here.
