Work / Robust classical visual front-end

Course project · Boğaziçi University · EE475 Image Processing · Sep – Oct 2026

When does classical image restoration help feature-based registration?

Target-only adaptive classical pre-processing for SIFT, RootSIFT, and ORB homography registration on HPatches. A four-gate blind spatial selector improves registration success by +33.0 pp on contrast compression and +15.1 pp on impulse noise, while a two-stage re-diagnosis cascade resolves compound degradations.

Platform
HPatches v1.1 benchmark sequences; OpenCV SIFT, RootSIFT and ORB with RANSAC homography estimation
Role
Individual research study: asymmetric target-only restoration architecture, four-gate blind spatial selector, detector failure-mode analysis, and two-stage compound-degradation recovery.
Key results
  • +33.0 pp SIFT registration gain at 3 px under contrast compression (0.418 → 0.748; +70.0 pp at α = 0.15)
  • +15.1 pp SIFT registration gain at 3 px under salt-and-pepper noise (0.575 → 0.726)
  • +32.4 pp two-stage re-diagnosis gain over single-stage selection under compound contrast + impulse corruption (0.471 → 0.795)
Modality
public benchmark datasets

Overview

Visual odometry and SLAM front-ends match local features between a clean reference keyframe (Iref) and an incoming target frame (Itgt) that may be degraded by sensor noise, impulse bit errors, poor exposure, or motion blur. Applying a fixed image-restoration filter unconditionally helps its target corruption family yet degrades clean and differently corrupted frames by smoothing or ringing the local gradients that feature detectors rely on[2]. This project formulates classical pre-processing as an asymmetric, target-only routing problem: the reference image Iref bypasses restoration, while Itgt is diagnosed from four fast spatial statistics and routed only when restoration improves downstream geometric registration.

Target-only four-gate blind selector

Four single-image spatial diagnostics are computed on Itgt in 11.4 ms (≈ 3.1 % of a 369 ms SIFT front-end) and evaluated in a priority cascade (Figure 1)[3][4]:

  1. Gate 1 — Impulse ratio Simp = fraction of saturated pixels (Itgt ≤ 2 or Itgt ≥ 253) > 0.005 → 3×3 median filter.
  2. Gate 2 — Percentile dynamic range Scon = P99(Itgt) − P1(Itgt) ≤ 120 DN → (1 %, 99 %) percentile contrast stretch.
  3. Gate 3 — Median-residual noise level Snoi = std(Itgt − Median3×3(Itgt)) > 8.0 DN → Gaussian low-pass smoothing (7×7 spatial kernel, reducing preprocessing latency by 90.5 % relative to FFT filtering with identical registration accuracy)[3].
  4. Gate 4 — Blur abstention Sblur = Var(∇²Itgt) / (Var(Itgt) + ε) ≤ 0.008 → pass-through, deliberately abstaining from blind sharpening because uncalibrated spatial sharpening amplifies edge ringing without inverting the unknown point-spread function[7].

Experimental evaluation across single and compound degradations

Across seven degradation families on HPatches (Figure 2), the blind adaptive selector matches the oracle family-aware upper bound within ±0.6 pp on every degradation family while routing 94 % of clean images to pass-through (0.0 pp change, 0.740 → 0.740)[1][5]:

  • Contrast compression (+33.0 pp): SIFT registration success rate (SR@3 px, mean corner transfer error ≤ 3 px) rises from 0.418 to 0.748 (95 % CI [+27.3, +38.8] pp), and hard failures fall from 45.3 % to 3.5 %. At severe contrast compression (α = 0.15), Difference-of-Gaussians extrema fall below SIFT’s internal contrast threshold τc = 0.04, collapsing median target keypoints from 3,928 to 5; (1 %, 99 %) percentile stretching restores the affine intensity slope, recovering 5,010 median keypoints, 592 inlier matches, and 76.0 % SR@3 (+70.0 pp; Figure 3)[5][6].
  • Salt-and-pepper impulse noise (+15.1 pp): SIFT SR@3 rises from 0.575 to 0.726 ([+10.4, +20.0] pp), scaling with impulse density up to +32.5 pp (0.385 → 0.710) at d = 0.20 while avoiding the −5.0 to −6.5 pp accuracy penalty that unconditional median filtering inflicts on clean and blurred images (Figure 4)[2][5].

Two-stage sequential re-diagnosis for compound corruption

When an image undergoes simultaneous contrast compression and salt-and-pepper noise (Icomp = SPd(Contrastα(I))), even 1 %–5 % impulse noise pins the 1st and 99th intensity percentiles near 0 and 255, inflating raw Scon to a median of 213.8 DN > 120 DN and masking contrast loss in 100 % of pairs[8]. Re-evaluating Gate 2 on the post-median image (median_3x3 -> stretch_p1_p99, zero additional parameters) drops the post-median dynamic range S′con to 60.2 DN ≤ 120 DN, deterministically triggering Stage 2 contrast stretching and improving SIFT SR@3 over single-stage selection by +32.4 pp under moderate compound corruption (0.471 → 0.795) and +77.6 pp under severe compound corruption (0.011 → 0.787; Figure 5)[8].
