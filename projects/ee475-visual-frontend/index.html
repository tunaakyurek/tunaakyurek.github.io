When does classical image restoration help feature-based registration?

Target-only adaptive classical pre-processing for SIFT, RootSIFT, and ORB homography registration on HPatches. A four-gate blind spatial selector frozen on 76 development sequences gained +33.0 pp on contrast loss and +15.1 pp on impulse noise on the 40 held-out test sequences, and a two-stage re-diagnosis extension resolved compound degradations.

Platform
HPatches v1.1 sequences (76 development, 40 held-out test); OpenCV SIFT, RootSIFT and ORB with MAGSAC++/RANSAC homographies
My role
Individual course project: target-only restoration architecture, four-gate blind selector, Phase I–III frozen test evaluation, and Phase IV two-stage compound-degradation extension.
Key results
  • +33.0 pp SIFT registration gain at 3 px under contrast loss on held-out TEST (0.418 → 0.748; +70.0 pp at α = 0.15)
  • +15.1 pp SIFT registration gain at 3 px under salt-and-pepper noise on held-out TEST (0.575 → 0.726)
  • +32.4 pp Phase IV two-stage re-diagnosis gain over single-stage v1 under compound contrast + impulse corruption (0.471 → 0.795)
Evidence
public benchmark datasets — headline numbers are labelled by split (76 DEV vs 40 held-out TEST); splits are never pooled.

The problem

Visual odometry and SLAM front-ends match local features between a stored reference frame (Iref) and an incoming target frame (Itgt) that may arrive corrupted by sensor noise, impulse bit errors, poor exposure, or blur. Applying a fixed restoration filter unconditionally can help its target degradation yet destroy the high-frequency gradient structure that detectors like SIFT and ORB rely on when the image is clean or differently degraded[2].

Target-only four-gate blind selector

I formulated classical pre-processing as an asymmetric, target-only routing problem (Figure 1): the clean reference image Iref is never filtered, while Itgt is diagnosed from four single-image spatial statistics computed in 11.4 ms (≈ 3.1 % of a 369 ms SIFT front-end)[3]:

  1. Gate 1 — Impulse ratio Simp = fraction of saturated pixels (Itgt ≤ 2 or Itgt ≥ 253) > 0.005 → 3×3 median filter.
  2. Gate 2 — Percentile dynamic range Scon = P99(Itgt) − P1(Itgt) ≤ 120 DN → (1 %, 99 %) percentile contrast stretch.
  3. Gate 3 — Median-residual noise level Snoi = std(Itgt − Median3×3(Itgt)) > 8.0 DN → Gaussian low-pass smoothing (D0 = 0.25 FFT Gaussian in adaptive_accuracy, or equivalent 7×7 spatial Gaussian σ = 1 px in adaptive_efficiency).
  4. Gate 4 — Normalized Laplacian sharpness Sblur = Var(∇²Itgt) / (Var(Itgt) + ε) ≤ 0.008 → pass-through (none), deliberately abstaining from blind sharpening because spatial sharpening amplifies edge ringing without inverting the unknown point-spread function.

All operator choices and gate thresholds were selected on 76 HPatches development sequences (split a, 5-fold sequence-grouped out-of-fold cross-validation, 3,610 pair evaluations per policy), locked in configs/frozen/protocol_v1.json, and evaluated once on the 40 held-out test sequences (200 pairs per severity cell, 92,200 pair evaluations across 461 frozen experiments)[4].

Held-out test results (Phase III)

On the once-evaluated 40-sequence TEST split, the blind selector (adaptive_accuracy) matches the family-aware upper bound within ±0.6 pp on every degradation family while leaving clean images untouched[5]:

  • Contrast loss: SIFT registration success rate (SR@3 px, mean corner transfer error ≤ 3 px) rises from 0.418 (none) to 0.748 (+33.0 pp, 95 % bootstrap CI [+27.3, +38.8] pp; family-aware 0.748). At the severest level (α = 0.15), SR@3 recovers from 0.060 to 0.760 (+70.0 pp), and contrast-loss hard failures (MCE > 10 px or no homography) fall from 45.3 % to 3.5 %[5].
  • Salt-and-pepper noise: SIFT SR@3 rises from 0.575 to 0.726 (+15.1 pp, [+10.4, +20.0] pp; family-aware 0.720), reaching +32.5 pp (0.385 → 0.710) at density d = 0.20[5].
  • Clean & secondary families: On clean test images (94 % routed to none), SR@3 is unchanged at 0.740 → 0.740 (0.0 pp, [−1.5, +1.5] pp). Small positive shifts on Gaussian noise (+0.8 pp, 0.691 → 0.699), gamma (+0.6 pp, 0.719 → 0.725), Gaussian blur (+0.6 pp), and motion blur (+0.3 pp) have confidence intervals spanning zero[5]. Pooled TEST hard-failure rate drops from 17.7 % to 11.6 %.

Why contrast compression breaks SIFT — and why percentile stretching fixes it

Although SIFT descriptors normalize local gradient vectors to unit ℓ₂ norm, SIFT keypoint detection discards Difference-of-Gaussian extrema with |D(x)| < τc = 0.04 before descriptor extraction. Under severe contrast compression (α = 0.15 on TEST), |D(x)| scales by α: median target SIFT keypoints collapse from 3,928 (clean) to 5 (63/200 pairs yield zero keypoints, and median ground-truth-consistent matches drop to 1)[6]. Applying (1 %, 99 %) percentile stretching restores the affine slope by (P99 − P1)−1, recovering 5,010 median keypoints and 592 ground-truth-consistent matches (Figure 4)[6].

Phase IV extension: compound degradations & two-stage sequential re-diagnosis

The Phase I–III selector returns a single operator per image. When an image suffers simultaneous contrast compression and salt-and-pepper noise (Icomp = SPd(Contrastα(I))), two coupled failure modes emerge under a single-stage cascade[8]:

  1. Diagnostic masking of Scon: Even 1 %–5 % salt-and-pepper impulses pin the 1st and 99th intensity percentiles near P1 = 0 and P99 = 255. On condition C3 (α = 0.3 + d = 0.05, 380 DEV pairs), the raw percentile spread has a median of 213.8 DN > 120 DN, masking severe contrast compression in 380/380 (100 %) of pairs[8].
  2. Single-operator insufficiency: Gate 1 correctly fires and applies the 3×3 median filter to strip the impulses, but the single-stage pipeline terminates and feeds the contrast-compressed median output directly to SIFT (SR@3 = 0.471 on C3 and 0.011 on C5 where α = 0.15 + d = 0.05).

Without changing any threshold or adding any parameter, I extended the selector to re-evaluate Gate 2 on the post-median image (adaptive_compound_v2): once Gate 1’s 3×3 median filter removes the impulses, the post-median dynamic range S′con = P99(I(1)) − P1(I(1)) drops to a median of 60.2 DN ≤ 120 DN on 380/380 C3 pairs, deterministically triggering Stage 2 (1 %, 99 %) contrast stretching (median_3x3 -> stretch_p1_p99)[8].

Across the preregistered 76-sequence Phase IV matrix (p4-compound-v1, 380 pairs per cell), adaptive_compound_v2 raises SIFT SR@3 over the single-stage v1 selector by +32.4 pp on C3 (α = 0.3 + d = 0.05: 0.471 → 0.795, CI [+25.5, +39.5] pp), +12.9 pp on C4 (α = 0.3 + d = 0.01: 0.658 → 0.787), and +77.6 pp on C5 (α = 0.15 + d = 0.05: 0.011 → 0.787, where v1 suffered 98.4 % hard failures), while remaining bit-identical on C0 clean (0.813) and C1 contrast-only (0.789) and within +0.8 pp on C2 impulse-only (0.789 vs 0.782)[8].

Limitations

Blind spatial sharpening does not recover blur because it cannot invert the unknown blur kernel; in a separate non-blind oracle PSF experiment on TEST, target-only Wiener deconvolution with the true kernel improved SIFT SR@3 by +18.4 pp on Gaussian blur (0.517 → 0.701) and +22.9 pp on motion blur (0.460 → 0.689), whereas symmetric Wiener deconvolution on both reference and target gained only +6.2 pp and +2.2 pp due to ringing on the clean reference image[7]. Raw HPatches images are not displayed here because their redistribution terms are unverified.

Phase II development split, severity-response curves, and unconditional-restoration harm
  • Development out-of-fold gains (Figure 7). On the 76 DEV sequences, adaptive_accuracy improved SIFT SR@3 by +36.1 pp on contrast loss (0.432 → 0.793, hard failures 0.454 → 0.046) and +16.2 pp on salt-and-pepper (0.598 → 0.761), recovering ρ = 1.00 and ρ = 0.98 of the family-aware upper bound[1][2].
  • Severity scaling on TEST (Figure 8). Across the three severity levels L₁, L₂, L₃, salt-and-pepper gains grow from +3.0 pp (d = 0.02) to +10.0 pp (d = 0.08) and +32.5 pp (d = 0.20); contrast-loss gains grow from +3.0 pp (α = 0.50) to +26.0 pp (α = 0.30) and +70.0 pp (α = 0.15)[5].
  • Why unconditional filtering fails (Figure 9). Applying 3×3 median filtering to every image gains +15.1 pp on salt-and-pepper but degrades clean images by −5.0 pp, Gaussian noise by −5.3 pp, and blur by −5.3 to −6.5 pp[2].
Selector routing matrix and spatial-vs-FFT Gaussian runtime ablation
  • Routing specificity (Figure 10). On held-out TEST, the blind selector routes 94 % of clean images, 97 % of Gaussian-blur images, and 92 % of motion-blur images to none, while routing 97 % of salt-and-pepper images to 3×3 median filtering and 99 % of contrast-loss images to (1 %, 99 %) percentile stretching[5].
  • Spatial vs FFT smoothing (Figure 11). Replacing Gate 3’s FFT Gaussian low-pass filter (adaptive_accuracy, 243.7 ms/pair on Gaussian noise) with an equivalent 7×7 spatial Gaussian kernel (adaptive_efficiency, 23.1 ms/pair) reduces preprocessing latency by 90.5 % and total front-end latency by 36.2 % with a −0.1 pp ([−2.6, +2.1] pp) difference in SIFT SR@3[3].
