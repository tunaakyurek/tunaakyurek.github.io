When does image restoration help feature matching?

SIFT and ORB image registration under controlled degradations, with a cheap blind selector that decides whether and how to restore an image before matching. Choices were frozen on 76 development sequences, then evaluated once on 40 held-out test sequences.

Platform
HPatches image sequences (76 development, 40 test); OpenCV SIFT, RootSIFT and ORB with RANSAC homographies
My role
Individual course project: pipeline, degradation and restoration operators, selector and evaluation protocol.
Key results
  • 0.43 → 0.79 SIFT registration success at 3 px under contrast loss, with the selector (development split)
  • +33.0 pp contrast-loss gain held on the once-evaluated test split (+36.1 pp on development)
Evidence
public benchmark datasets — headline numbers are labelled by split; development and test sequences are never pooled.

The problem

Visual odometry and SLAM front-ends match features between images, and real images arrive noisy, blurred, dim or washed out. Restoring an image first can help — or destroy the texture the detector needs.

What I did

I measured when restoration helps SIFT, RootSIFT and ORB homography registration under six degradation families, and built a blind selector: four cheap image statistics with gated thresholds that choose whether to restore and with which filter, without being told the degradation. All choices were made on 76 HPatches development sequences with 5-fold out-of-fold testing, then frozen[4].

Result

In development, under contrast loss, the selector raised SIFT’s success rate at 3 px from 0.43 to 0.79 and cut hard failures from 45 % to 5 %[1]. For impulse noise and contrast loss it recovered 98–100 % of the gain available from a restoration matched to the known degradation; for Gaussian noise 68 %, for gamma 22 %, and blur was not helped at all[2]. The diagnostics add 11.4 ms, about 3 % of the front-end’s runtime[3].

On the held-out test split, evaluated once with the frozen protocol, the large gains held: +15.1 percentage points for impulse noise and +33.0 for contrast loss, against +16.2 and +36.1 in development. The small development gain for Gaussian noise was not resolved on test[5].

Limitation

Blur is not helped and the gamma gain is small; two selected thresholds sit at the edge of their search grid. Dataset images are not shown here because their redistribution terms are unverified.
