Work / Quadruped exploration & return-home

Independent project · simulation (Isaac Sim, ROS 2) · Autumn 2026

A simulated quadruped that explores, finds landmarks and returns home

A simulated Unitree Go2 explores a building it has never seen, localises five coloured flags as 3-D landmarks and walks back to its start. Frozen and tested on 40 held-out worlds, it returned home in 39.

Platform
Unitree Go2 model in Isaac Sim with simulated LiDAR, RGB-D camera and IMU; autonomy stack in ROS 2
My role
Mission and acceptance criteria, system integration, evaluation protocol, experiments and the frozen benchmark.
Key results
  • 39 / 40 held-out missions that returned home (95 % CI 0.87–1.00)
  • 0.95 / 0.97 landmark recall / precision, pooled over the 40 missions

The problem

Every component a legged robot needs for this mission already exists — locomotion, LiDAR-inertial SLAM, terrain mapping, path planning, open-vocabulary detection. The hard part is making them run together, unattended, every time, and measuring that honestly. Everything here is simulation; no physical robot was used[7].

What I did

I specified the mission and the acceptance gates, chose the evaluation protocol, ran the experiments and the benchmarks, and analysed them. The parts specific to this project are a terrain-to-occupancy bridge, a semantic mapper that turns detections into 3-D landmarks, a mission executive, and a bounded return-home supervisor with a stall watchdog, limited retries and a bias towards the path already walked. Third-party components (GLIM, Nav2, Grounding DINO + SAM 2) are pinned and unmodified.

Result

The system was frozen at one commit and run once on each of 40 worlds that were never used during development, with a 600 s budget per mission.

It returned home in 39 of 40 missions; 25 on the first attempt and 14 after a supervised retry. The one failure was a fall[1]. Mean coverage was 0.856 and mean SLAM trajectory error 0.085 m; six missions had at least one collision[2].

An earlier version returned home in 12 of 20 missions, but on a different set of worlds — so the two numbers are not a controlled before/after comparison[3].

Limitations

Simulation only, one building generator and one run per world. SLAM error rose relative to the earlier version with no cause established, and no transfer to a real robot has been attempted.

Architecture, locomotion and development experiments

Locomotion. The walking policy is a fine-tune of NVIDIA’s pretrained Isaac Lab Go2 policy with added posture and contact terms, not trained from scratch. On a held-out split of 48 seeds × 59 scenarios, falls dropped from 64 to 4 of 2,832 episodes and zero-command yaw drift from 49.7° to 0.8° per 30 s[4]. It still pitches over on 3 of 48 seeds descending a 20° slope.

Return policy. On 12 development worlds run with each policy, a single-shot return succeeded 8/12 and retry plus the walked-path bias 12/12. A rule written down before the runs picked the latter; the difference is not statistically significant at this sample size[5].

Learned frontier selector. A PPO selector trained in a 2-D surrogate showed no detectable difference from the heuristic in 10 paired missions, so the heuristic was kept[6].
