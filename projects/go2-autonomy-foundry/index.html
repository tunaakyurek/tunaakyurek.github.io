Work / Quadruped exploration & return-home

Independent research · simulation (Isaac Sim, ROS 2) · Autumn 2026

Closed-Loop Quadruped Exploration, Semantic Landmark Mapping, and Return-to-Origin in Simulation

A simulated Unitree Go2 quadruped explores unseen indoor buildings, localizes five semantic target flags in 3-D, and returns to its starting pose. Following closed-loop failure diagnosis and locomotion stabilization, the closed system returned home in 30 of 30 unseen worlds where the inherited one managed 21.

Platform
Unitree Go2 quadruped in Isaac Sim with 3D LiDAR, RGB-D camera and IMU; ROS 2 autonomy stack
Role
System architecture, closed-loop failure diagnosis, locomotion policy stabilization, semantic landmark pipeline, and multi-world statistical evaluation.
Key results
  • 30/30 vs 21/30 returned home on the same 30 unseen simulated worlds (closed vs inherited system)
  • 31/36 returned home across three unseen simulated building layouts
  • 39/40 returned home in a 40-world evaluation campaign (95 % CI 0.87–1.00)
Evaluation modality
simulation — simulation only (NVIDIA Isaac Sim); no physical Unitree Go2 hardware experiments were conducted.

The video above shows a complete autonomous mission from startup through frontier exploration, 3-D semantic target localization, and return-to-origin, confirming 5 of 5 semantic targets with zero falls and stopping 0.19 m from the start pose[14].

Overview

Autonomous indoor inspection with a legged robot requires coupling learned rough-terrain locomotion, 3-D LiDAR-inertial SLAM, GPU elevation mapping, global and local path planning, and open-vocabulary semantic perception into a single unattended loop[7]. While each subsystem can perform well in isolation, closed-loop missions frequently fail at subsystem boundaries—such as middleware lifecycle synchronization, transient costmap corruption from leg or step-edge returns, and locomotion contact-parameter mismatches. This project investigates how targeted system-level supervision and locomotion stabilization close those reliability gaps across large-scale procedural building benchmarks in simulation.

System architecture & reliability closure

The autonomy stack runs across three isolated runtime layers: Isaac Sim physics and sensor simulation, a ROS 2 autonomy container (GLIM 3-D LiDAR-inertial SLAM, elevation_mapping_cupy, Nav2 with SmacPlanner2D and MPPI, frontier extraction, and the mission executive), and a semantic perception container (Grounding DINO + SAM 2 with multi-view 3-D landmark confirmation)[7]. Diagnostic analysis of initial mission failures (12/20 returns in an early unpaired baseline campaign on seeds r200–r219[3]) motivated three targeted architectural additions:

  • Bounded startup lifecycle probe. Replaced single-shot middleware service waits during Nav2 activation with a deadline-driven retry and exponential back-off supervisor.
  • Supervised return-to-origin with traversed-trail prior. Because the 2-D navigation costmap is continuously regenerated from the 3-D terrain map at 2 Hz, standard Nav2 costmap clearing cannot remove persistent false obstacles on the return path. A bounded return supervisor combines a stall watchdog (up to three recovery attempts within a fixed time budget) with a traversed-trail prior that clears cells the robot physically walked through during outbound exploration[11].
  • Locomotion friction alignment & constrained PPO continuation. Auditing the deployed Isaac Lab rough-terrain locomotion policy revealed that training-time contact-friction randomization had been omitted in the deployment environment. Restoring matched friction parameters and running a constrained PPO continuation with upright posture, thigh-contact, and zero-velocity yaw penalties stabilized standing posture (25.4 cm → 32.5 cm stand height; 23 % → 0 % thigh contact; Figure 8)[4].

Experimental evaluation

Paired 30-world system comparison

Because the initial 12/20 baseline run (r200–r219) and the 39/40 reliability campaign (r500–r539) were evaluated on different world sets[3], a controlled paired evaluation ran both the baseline (inherited) stack and the reliability-closed stack on the same 30 procedurally generated worlds (r600–r629) with identical seeds[9].

The baseline system returned home in 21/30 worlds, whereas the reliability-closed system returned home in 30/30 worlds. All 9 discordant pairs favoured the closed system (exact McNemar p = 0.0039; paired risk difference +0.30, 95 % CI 0.12–0.48)[9]. Paired SLAM trajectory error remained consistent across identical worlds (0.07 m vs 0.075 m, difference +0.005 m)[12]. In the separate 40-world evaluation campaign (r500–r539), the closed system returned home in 39/40 missions (25 on the first attempt and 14 via supervised retry; Figure 7)[1].

Cross-layout generalization & recovery ablation

To test generalization beyond the base floor-plan family, the system was evaluated across three distinct architectural topologies—looped offices, open-aisle warehouses, and irregular multi-room buildings (12 worlds per family, 36 total).

The system returned home in 31/36 missions across the primary set, and 32/36 on the geometry-corrected set after resolving a spawn-clearance artifact in three worlds[10]. In a dedicated 20-world return-recovery ablation (r700–r719), single-shot return succeeded in 13/20 missions, watchdog retry in 14/20, and watchdog retry with the traversed-trail prior in 20/20 (exact McNemar p = 0.031, Holm-adjusted p = 0.062, indicating a positive trend that does not cross the pre-registered α = 0.05 threshold at n = 20)[11]. A learned PPO frontier selector was also benchmarked against the deterministic cost-utility heuristic across 10 paired missions, showing comparable mission performance and confirming the effectiveness of the deterministic planner[6].

Locomotion stabilization & semantic perception

Across 2,832 evaluation episodes (48 seeds × 59 scenarios), locomotion falls went from 64 to 8 when aligning the friction configuration and to 4 with the stabilised policy, while zero-command yaw drift dropped from 50° to 1° per 30 s[4]. Across the 40-world campaign (200 total targets), semantic landmark precision reached 0.974 and recall 0.95[2]: every target observed long enough was detected and confirmed in 3-D, while the 10 missing flags were never in view during frontier exploration[13][15].

Limitations & scope

  • Simulation-only evaluation: All experiments were conducted in NVIDIA Isaac Sim 5.1; no physical Unitree Go2 hardware deployment or sim-to-real transfer trials were performed.
  • Semantic false positives in the paired study: A relaxed HSV color-ratio threshold in the closed configuration produced false-positive landmark confirmations in 4/30 paired missions (vs. 0/30 in the baseline configuration and 5/195 confirmations in the 40-world campaign)[2][9][15].
  • Cross-layout collisions and residual controller stalls: On the 36-world cross-layout suite, return reliability was lower (31/36 primary, 32/36 geometry-corrected) than on the base building family (30/30), with body/leg contact events recorded in 12/36 missions and residual MPPI local-planner stalls near narrow doorways and step edges[10][15].
  • Visibility-bounded recall: Because the frontier planner optimizes geometric map coverage rather than camera view planning, 10 of 200 semantic targets in the 40-world campaign never entered the RGB-D frustum (190/200 visible, 190/190 confirmed)[13][15].
