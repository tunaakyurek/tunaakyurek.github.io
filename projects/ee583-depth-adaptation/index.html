Work / Metric depth adaptation study

Course project · Boğaziçi University · EE583 Deep Learning · Sep 2026

How much of a depth foundation model should you fine-tune?

A controlled study of partial fine-tuning of Depth Anything V2-Small for indoor metric depth. Adapting the head and last six blocks matched full fine-tuning on NYUv2 — but made absolute scale worse on an unseen benchmark.

Platform
Depth Anything V2-Small (ViT-S/14 + DPT); NYU Depth V2 in domain, iBims-1 out of domain
My role
Individual course project: experiment design, training and evaluation pipeline, statistics and write-up.
Key results
  • 0.214 → 0.090 NYUv2 test AbsRel, zero-shot → selected model (3 seeds)
  • 0.614 → 0.776 m RMSE on the unseen iBims-1 benchmark: adaptation hurt transfer
Evidence
public benchmark datasets — one backbone size and indoor datasets only; no state-of-the-art claim.

The problem

A robot that needs metres, not relative depth, usually starts from a pretrained network and fine-tunes it on its own environment. How much of the network should be unfrozen — and what does in-domain accuracy cost elsewhere?

What I did

I trained a ladder of adaptation depths for Depth Anything V2-Small — zero-shot, zero-shot with one global scale factor, the head only, the head plus the last 3, 6 or 9 transformer blocks, and full fine-tuning — each with three seeds on NYUv2. Choices were made on validation. The zero-shot, scale-calibrated, head-only and selected six-block models were each evaluated once on the official NYUv2 test set, and once on iBims-1, a benchmark none of them saw in training; the 3-, 9-block and full fine-tuning runs are validation evidence only.

Result

Training the head plus the last six blocks (13.4 M of 24.8 M parameters) matched full fine-tuning on validation[3]. On the NYUv2 test set it cut AbsRel from 0.214 to 0.090 and RMSE from 0.610 m to 0.319 m[1]. On iBims-1, however, its RMSE got worse than the zero-shot model’s, 0.776 m against 0.614 m: the network lost part of its sense of absolute scale in new rooms. Interpolating between the adapted and original weights recovered about half of that loss[2].

Limitation

The cause of the out-of-domain scale drift was not isolated, and the interpolated model was not evaluated on the NYUv2 test set.

Adaptation ladder on validation, protocol and inference cost
  • Splits. NYUv2 was split scene-disjointly into 643 training and 152 validation images. The official 654-image test set was used once per frozen configuration.
  • Training. SILog loss (λ = 0.5), AdamW with layer-wise learning rates (5 × 10⁻⁶ encoder, 5 × 10⁻⁵ head), 40 epochs, 518 px crops, three seeds per configuration.
  • Statistics. Confidence intervals from a scene-cluster bootstrap; an equal-epoch control ruled out training length as the reason six blocks beat the head alone.
  • Inference cost. 17.8 ms fp32 and 14.5 ms fp16, model only, at 518 × 686 on an RTX 4070 Ti SUPER[4].
