Course project · Boğaziçi University · EE583 Deep Learning · Sep – Oct 2026

Metric Adaptation and Cross-Camera Generalization of Depth Anything V2 Across Indoor Sensors

Controlled metric adaptation of Depth-Anything-V2-Metric-Hypersim (Small and Base) across nine indoor benchmarks. On NYUv2, full fine-tuning reduces AbsRel from 0.214 to 0.082 (0.075 for Base), and rank-32 LoRA reaches 0.083 using 21% of the parameters while cross-domain evaluation across six external cameras characterizes source scale transfer.

Platform
Depth-Anything-V2-Metric-Hypersim Small (ViT-S/14, 24.8 M) and Base (ViT-B/14, 97.5 M); NYUv2, SUN RGB-D, iBims-1, DIODE Indoor, TUM RGB-D, and ARKitScenes
Role
Individual research study: 14-point adaptation-depth sweep, LoRA/L2-SP/WiSE-FT regularization analysis, multi-source camera transfer experiments, and statistical evaluation.
Key results
  • 0.214 → 0.082 NYUv2 AbsRel, pretrained Z → Small full fine-tuning F (0.281 m RMSE; 0.075 AbsRel / 0.248 m for Base F)
  • 0.083 (21 % params) NYUv2 AbsRel with rank-32 LoRA + head (L-r32, 5.09 M params, 80 % less AdamW state, zero inference overhead)
  • 9 benchmarks cross-camera evaluation across NYUv2, SUN RGB-D (4 sensors), iBims-1, DIODE, TUM, and ARKitScenes
Evaluation modality
public benchmark datasets — evaluated on nine public indoor RGB-D, laser-scanned, and mobile-LiDAR benchmarks (0.1–10 m range).

Overview

Robotic perception and 3-D mapping stacks require calibrated metric depth in metres rather than scale-ambiguous relative disparity. Adapting a metric foundation model such as Depth-Anything-V2-Metric-Hypersim (d̂ = 20 m · σ(·)) to a target indoor camera raises three core questions: (1) how much of the vision transformer backbone must be unfrozen—or adapted via low-rank adapters—to capture full fine-tuning accuracy; (2) how adaptation on one indoor camera transfers to unseen structured-light, time-of-flight, laser-scanned, and mobile-LiDAR sensors; and (3) what physical or statistical mechanism governs cross-camera scale shifts.

Adaptation regimes & experimental protocol

All configurations were trained on the 795 official NYUv2 training frames (249 scenes) for 40 epochs using scale-sensitive SiLog loss (λ = 0.5) and evaluated in raw metres ([0.1, 10.0] m) without per-image median alignment across nine indoor benchmark columns: NYUv2 (654 test frames), SUN RGB-D (4,396 non-NYUv2 frames stratified into B3DO Kinect v1, Xtion, Kinect v2, and RealSense), iBims-1 (100 laser-scanned frames), DIODE Indoor (325 laser-scanned frames), TUM RGB-D (223 frames), and ARKitScenes (804 mobile-LiDAR frames across 87 homes)[1][2][5]. Primary Small configurations were trained across 5 random seeds and LoRA / Base configurations across 3 seeds, with paired frame-level differences verified via 10,000-replicate scene-block bootstrap confidence intervals[1].

In-domain accuracy, LoRA efficiency, and backbone scale

Across the 14-point adaptation-depth sweep (H, contiguous top encoder blocks k = 1…12, and full fine-tuning F; Figure 3), unfreezing the top six encoder blocks (P-k6, 13.38 M parameters, 3.1 GiB peak VRAM) captures 92 % of the validation AbsRel reduction from head-only H (0.1206) to F (0.0979) at 61 % of the training memory[3]. On the NYUv2 test benchmark:

  • Full fine-tuning (F, Small ViT-S/14) reduces AbsRel from 0.2138 to 0.0820 and RMSE from 0.610 m to 0.281 m (δ₁: 0.675 → 0.948), eliminating the pretrained model’s +0.157 signed log bias[1].
  • Rank-32 LoRA + DPT head (L-r32) adapts all 12 encoder blocks using 21 % of the trainable parameters (5.09 M vs 24.78 M) and 80 % less AdamW optimizer state (38 MiB vs 189 MiB), achieving 0.0825 AbsRel and 0.283 m RMSE (δ₁ = 0.946). Merging the low-rank adapters into the linear layers after training preserves the exact 17.9 ms FP32 (14.5 ms FP16) inference latency per frame[4].
  • Backbone scaling (Base, ViT-B/14, 97.47 M parameters) reaches 0.0746 AbsRel and 0.248 m RMSE (δ₁ = 0.961), improving over F, Small by −0.0074 AbsRel at 2.4× the training compute and 2.0× the peak memory[1][4].

Cross-domain transfer & source calibration law

Evaluating all adapted models across nine indoor camera benchmarks (Figure 4) and repeating the 795-frame adaptation recipe across three distinct source cameras—NYUv2, SUN Kinect v2, and DIODE Indoor (Figure 5)—establishes a clear physical mechanism governing cross-camera transfer[2][5]:

  1. Global log-scale shift governed by source bias: Fine-tuning removes the pretrained model’s signed log bias on the training source (−bZ(S)) and applies a nearly uniform log-depth shift of the same sign and magnitude across target cameras (Figures 7–8). Because Z over-predicts depth on NYUv2 (+0.157) and SUN Kinect v2 (+0.175), adapting on either source strongly improves all Kinect-family sensors (SUN RGB-D pooled AbsRel 0.218 → 0.135; SUN Kinect v2 0.264 → 0.119), TUM RGB-D (0.223 → 0.201), and close-range ARKitScenes (0.537 → 0.359–0.521), while shifting already-calibrated DIODE Indoor (−0.049 → −0.229) into negative bias[2][5].
  2. Weight anchoring & LoRA regularize cross-camera shift: When deploying to cameras with different baseline calibration (such as laser-scanned iBims-1; Figure 6), WiSE-FT (F-W, 0.655 m RMSE), L2-SP anchoring (F-L2SP, 0.680 m), and rank-32 LoRA (L-r32, 0.733 m) mitigate the cross-domain RMSE shift compared to unconstrained full fine-tuning (F, 0.760 m)[2].

Limitations & scope

  • Indoor metric evaluation range: All experiments are bounded to indoor RGB-D, laser-scanned, and mobile-LiDAR benchmarks within [0.1, 10.0] m; outdoor long-range depth estimation is outside the scope of this study[1].
  • Camera intrinsics and scale ambiguity without intrinsics conditioning: Because monocular metric depth prediction couples scene geometry with camera focal length, unconstrained fine-tuning (F) or high-rank LoRA (L-r32) is optimal when deployment cameras share the training source’s scale bias, whereas weight-space interpolation (F-W), L2-SP anchoring (F-L2SP), or head-only adaptation (H) is preferable when the target sensor is already calibrated near zero log bias relative to the pretrained backbone[2][5].
