Work / Metric depth adaptation study

Course project · Boğaziçi University · EE583 Deep Learning · Sep – Oct 2026

Metric adaptation of Depth Anything V2 across indoor cameras

Controlled metric adaptation of Depth-Anything-V2-Metric-Hypersim (Small and Base) across nine indoor benchmarks. On NYUv2 test, full fine-tuning cuts AbsRel from 0.214 to 0.082 (0.075 for Base), and rank-32 LoRA reaches 0.083 with 21% of the parameters — while cross-domain evaluation across six external cameras exposes how source scale bias governs transfer.

Platform
Depth-Anything-V2-Metric-Hypersim Small (ViT-S/14, 24.8 M) and Base (ViT-B/14, 97.5 M); NYUv2, SUN RGB-D (4 sensors), iBims-1, DIODE Indoor, TUM RGB-D, and untouched ARKitScenes
My role
Individual course project: preregistered protocol v4, 14-point adaptation sweep, LoRA/L2-SP/WiSE-FT regularizers, multi-source transfer experiments, and statistical audit.
Key results
  • 0.214 → 0.082 NYUv2 test AbsRel, pretrained Z → Small full fine-tuning F (0.281 m RMSE; 0.075 AbsRel / 0.248 m for Base F)
  • 0.083 (21 % params) NYUv2 test AbsRel with rank-32 LoRA + head (L-r32, 5.09 M params, 80 % less AdamW state, zero inference overhead)
  • 6 external cameras cross-domain audit (SUN RGB-D, iBims-1, DIODE, TUM, and untouched ARKitScenes) isolating source log-scale transfer
Evidence
public benchmark datasets — indoor metric depth benchmarks only (0.1–10 m); all test evaluations gated by committed SHA-256 pre-evaluation locks.

The problem

A robotics stack that needs metric depth in metres—not relative disparity—typically starts from a metric foundation model such as Depth-Anything-V2-Metric-Hypersim (d̂ = 20 m · σ(·)) and adapts it to a target indoor camera. Doing so raises three practical engineering questions:

  1. How much of the network should be trained? Is adapting the DPT head (H) or the last k encoder blocks (P-k) enough, can LoRA (L-r32) match full fine-tuning (F) at a fraction of the trainable parameters, and how much does a 4× larger ViT-B/14 (Base) encoder add?
  2. What happens on other indoor cameras after adaptation? Does adapting on NYUv2 help or hurt unseen structured-light, time-of-flight, laser-scanned, and mobile-LiDAR benchmarks?
  3. What mechanism drives cross-camera transfer and failure? Can weight anchoring (L2-SP, WiSE-FT) or low-rank adaptation preserve out-of-domain accuracy without sacrificing in-domain calibration?

Experimental design (Protocol v4)

Under a preregistered protocol (protocol v4) with SHA-256 pre-evaluation locks and an append-only evaluation ledger, I trained every configuration on all 795 official NYUv2 training frames (249 scenes) for 40 epochs (7,920 steps of batch 4, 518×518 crops, FP32 with TF32 off, AdamW with 5×10⁻⁶ encoder LR and 5×10⁻⁵ head LR, scale-sensitive log loss λ = 0.5, last checkpoint)[1]. Hyperparameters were selected on training data only (5-fold scene-grouped cross-validation for L2-SP μ = 10⁻³ and WiSE α = 0.65; held-out training scenes across ranks r ∈ [4, 8, 16, 32] for LoRA r = 32). Every selected configuration was refitted with 5 seeds (Small) or 3 seeds (LoRA, Base), locked, and evaluated once in raw metres (no per-image median scaling) on:

  • In-domain: the official 654-frame NYUv2 test set[1].
  • Cross-domain (8 external columns): SUN RGB-D (4,396 non-NYU frames stratified by sensor into B3DO Kinect v1, Xtion, Kinect v2, and RealSense), iBims-1 (100 laser-scanned frames), DIODE Indoor (325 laser-scanned frames), TUM RGB-D (fr3, 223 frames), and ARKitScenes (804 mobile-LiDAR frames across 87 homes — never read before the final evaluation)[2][5].

In-domain accuracy, LoRA efficiency, and backbone scale

Across the 14-point NYUv2 validation sweep (H, last k = 1…12 encoder blocks, F; Figure 3), unfreezing the last six blocks (P-k6, 13.38 M parameters, 3.1 GiB peak memory) captures 92 % of the validation AbsRel reduction from H (0.1206) to F (0.0979) at 61 % of the training memory[3]. On the 654-frame NYUv2 test set (Table below and Figure 4):

  • Full fine-tuning (F, final Small model) cuts NYUv2 test AbsRel from 0.2138 to 0.0820 and RMSE from 0.610 m to 0.281 m (δ₁: 0.675 → 0.948), removing the pretrained model’s +0.157 signed log bias (+0.017 after adaptation) and beating P-k6 (0.0866 AbsRel, 0.296 m RMSE) by a confirmed paired difference of −0.0046 ([−0.0067, −0.0026])[1].
  • Rank-32 LoRA + head (L-r32) adapts all 12 encoder blocks (attn.qkv, attn.proj, mlp.fc1, mlp.fc2) plus the DPT head using only 21 % of the trainable parameters (5.09 M vs 24.78 M) and 80 % less AdamW optimizer state (38 MiB vs 189 MiB). It reaches 0.0825 AbsRel and 0.283 m RMSE (δ₁ = 0.946), beating head-only H by −0.0217 AbsRel (confirmed) and matching F within +0.0004 ([−0.0006, +0.0015], unresolved). Merging the rank-32 adapters into the linear layers after training changes predictions by at most 1.4×10⁻⁶ m, preserving the exact 17.9 ms FP32 (14.5 ms FP16) inference latency per 518×686 frame[4].
  • Scaling to Base (ViT-B/14, 97.47 M parameters): Pretrained Z, Base (0.2162 AbsRel) is no better than Z, Small (0.2138) before adaptation. After adaptation, F, Base reaches 0.0746 AbsRel and 0.248 m RMSE (δ₁ = 0.961), improving over F, Small by −0.0074 AbsRel (confirmed) at 2.4× the training time (49 vs 21 GPU-min, 358 vs 144 ms/step) and 2.0× the peak memory (10.3 vs 5.1 GiB)[1][4].

Cross-domain evaluation across nine indoor benchmarks

Evaluating all eight NYUv2-adapted configurations across the nine benchmark columns (Figure 5) reveals a consistent domain-transfer pattern[2][5]:

  1. Sensors in the same family benefit strongly: On SUN RGB-D (4,396 non-NYU frames), F cuts pooled AbsRel from 0.218 to 0.135 (RMSE 0.554 → 0.370 m), with the largest gain on SUN Kinect v2 (0.264 → 0.119 AbsRel, Δ = −0.145) and SUN Xtion (0.194 → 0.141). On TUM RGB-D, F improves AbsRel from 0.223 to 0.201[2].
  2. Laser-scanned benchmarks suffer from source scale transfer: Pretrained Z over-predicts NYUv2 depth (signed log bias +0.157), whereas on iBims-1 (+0.084) and DIODE Indoor (−0.049) its scale bias is already small or negative. Removing the +0.157 NYUv2 bias shifts predictions closer across all cameras: on DIODE Indoor, signed log bias shifts from −0.049 to −0.229 and AbsRel worsens from 0.264 to 0.296; on iBims-1, bias flips from +0.084 to −0.044 and RMSE rises from 0.614 m to 0.760 m[2].
  3. Anchoring and LoRA reduce the external RMSE penalty: On iBims-1, WiSE-FT (F-W) holds RMSE to 0.655 m (0.140 AbsRel), L2-SP (F-L2SP) reaches 0.680 m (0.146 AbsRel), and LoRA (L-r32) reduces RMSE to 0.733 m (0.152 AbsRel; confirmed −0.027 m improvement over F)[2].
  4. Untouched ARKitScenes benchmark (804 frames, 87 homes): On close-range ARKitScenes frames (median depth 1.39 m), pretrained Z severely over-predicts depth (0.537 AbsRel, +0.431 log bias). Every NYUv2-adapted model reduces this error: F reaches 0.521 AbsRel, L-r32 0.489, H 0.419, F-L2SP 0.387, and H, Base 0.359[5].

Limitations

All experiments evaluate indoor scenes within 0.1–10 m on two DINOv2 backbone sizes (Small and Base). Because monocular metric depth is inherently tied to the training camera’s focal length and sensor bias, no single NYUv2-adapted checkpoint dominates every external camera simultaneously: F and L-r32 are best when the deployment sensor matches the training camera family, whereas F-L2SP, F-W, or H are safer when the deployment camera is unknown.

Depth-resolved log bias and multi-source adaptation transfer (NYUv2, SUN Kinect v2, DIODE Indoor)
  • Depth-resolved log bias (Figure 6). Plotting mean ln(d̂/d) against ground-truth depth shows that NYUv2 adaptation shifts log depth downward across the entire 1–8 m range on every dataset; L2-SP and WiSE-FT pull the curve partially back toward Z.
  • Multi-source adaptation experiment (Figures 7 & 8). Repeating the exact 795-frame adaptation recipe on SUN Kinect v2 and DIODE Indoor confirms that cross-domain transfer is governed by the source camera’s initial bias bZ(S): SUN Kinect v2 adaptation (bZ = +0.175) transfers strongly to NYUv2 (−0.095 AbsRel) and untouched ARKitScenes (−0.179 for F, −0.240 for F-L2SP), whereas DIODE Indoor adaptation (bZ = −0.049) improves only DIODE (−0.020 AbsRel) and degrades every other benchmark by +0.16 to +0.52 AbsRel[5].
