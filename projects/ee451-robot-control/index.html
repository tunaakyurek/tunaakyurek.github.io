Work / Robot control: arms and mobile robots

Coursework · Boğaziçi University · EE451 Introduction to Robot Control · Fall 2025 – Jan 2026

Manipulator kinematics, motion planning and mobile robot autonomy

A five-module ROS and C++ robotics sequence spanning analytical and numerical inverse kinematics, Jacobian velocity-level pick-and-place among obstacles for 3-DOF manipulators in Gazebo, and A*, Pure Pursuit, and Dynamic Window autonomous exploration on a physical TurtleBot.

Platform
3-DOF RRP and RRR spatial manipulators in Gazebo; differential-drive TurtleBot in Gazebo and a 3 m × 3 m laboratory arena
Role
Modules 1–4 designed and implemented individually. Module 5 (autonomous arena exploration) architected and implemented as lead developer in a three-person team.
Modality
simulation · hardware

Spatial manipulators: kinematics, planning & control (Modules 1–3)

The first three modules developed complete URDF models, kinematic solvers, and joint-space controllers for 3-DOF spatial manipulators in ROS Noetic and Gazebo[1][2]:

  • Modules 1–2 — RRP & RRR forward/inverse kinematics and trajectory tracking. Derived closed-form geometric inverse kinematics alongside damped least-squares Jacobian pseudo-inverse numerical solvers, workspace reachability envelopes, and quintic polynomial joint-space trajectory tracking to reach 3-D target poses (Figure 2).
  • Module 3 — Velocity-level pick-and-place among obstacles. Formulated full analytical Jacobians for the RRP arm under damped least-squares inverse-velocity control, combined with a 3-D configuration-space Dijkstra path planner with obstacle inflation and Artificial Potential Field (APF) repulsive guidance across lift, obstacle-clearing transport, and placement phases (Figure 3).

Mobile robot planning & physical arena autonomy (Modules 4–5)

The second half of the sequence transitioned to a differential-drive TurtleBot (Figure 1), validating planning and control pipelines in Gazebo (Figure 4) before deploying onto the physical laboratory robot inside a 3 m × 3 m arena equipped with ceiling-mounted overhead fiducial localization (Figure 5)[6]:

  • Module 4 — Multi-goal delivery with A and Pure Pursuit + APF.* Implemented exact-arc differential-drive odometry integration, 2-D occupancy-grid A* path planning with C-space obstacle inflation, optimal target-visiting order search over pairwise A* path costs, and Pure Pursuit lookahead tracking fused with repulsive APF obstacle avoidance[3][2]. To accommodate the 100–200 ms transport latency of the overhead camera localization feed on hardware, angular velocity commands were bounded at 1.0 rad/s to preserve closed-loop phase margin[5].
  • Module 5 — Autonomous arena exploration and visual target inspection. Designed a state-machine mission executive integrating a latency-compensating pose estimator, 0.8 m lawnmower coverage planner, smoothed A* global planner, and Dynamic Window local planner to autonomously map unknown cylindrical obstacles, position the robot at 0.30 m inspection viewpoints around colour-coded targets, and return within 0.2 m of the starting pose[4][7].
